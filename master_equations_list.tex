Master equations (deterministic rate equations) for the aortic-matrix cascade
These are the governing ordinary differential equations that describe the time evolution of the key concentrations under the intestinal dysbiosis (TMAO/LPS) ⊕ ROS/HIF-1α pathway leading to aortic extracellular-matrix (ECM) degradation.
State variables
(T) = [TMAO]
(L) = [LPS]
(R) = [ROS] (effective concentration, mainly \(\mathrm{O_2^{\bullet-}}\) / \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}\))
(H) = [active HIF-1α]
(M) = [MMP activity] (primarily MMP-2/9)
(E) = aortic ECM integrity (elastin + collagen mass fraction)
(D) = dysbiosis drive (external input / parameter)
Complete list of equations from the aortic-cavitation / gut–heart–lung (tropism) model and its quantitative couplings.

1. Repository master biochemical ODEs
\[
\begin{align*}
\frac{dT}{dt} &= k_T D - \delta_T T \\
\frac{dL}{dt} &= k_L D - \delta_L L \\
\frac{dR}{dt} &= \alpha_T T + \alpha_L L - \delta_R R \\
\frac{dH}{dt} &= \beta R (H_{\max} - H) - \delta_H H \\
\frac{dM}{dt} &= \gamma_H H + \gamma_R R - \delta_M M \\
\frac{dE}{dt} &= p - \mu M E
\end{align*}
\]

2. Extended biochemical ODEs (with mechanotransduction + cavitation)
\[
\begin{align*}
\frac{dT}{dt} &= k_T D - \delta_T T \\
\frac{dL}{dt} &= k_L D - \delta_L L \\
\frac{dR}{dt} &= \alpha_T T + \alpha_L L + \alpha_\tau \overline{\tau}w + \alpha\sigma \overline{\sigma} - \delta_R R \\
\frac{dH}{dt} &= \beta R (H_{\max} - H) - \delta_H H \\
\frac{dM}{dt} &= \gamma_H H + \gamma_R R + \gamma_\varepsilon \phi(\overline{\varepsilon}) - \delta_M M \\
\frac{dE}{dt} &= p - \mu M E - \kappa\, q(\text{cavitation indicator})
\end{align*}
\]

3. Analytic Biochemical Jacobian matrix
\[
J = \frac{\partial\mathbf{f}}{\partial\mathbf{y}} =
\begin{pmatrix}
-\delta_T & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -\delta_L & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\alpha_T & \alpha_L & -\delta_R & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \beta(H_{\max}-H) & -\beta R-\delta_H & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \gamma_R & \gamma_H & -\delta_M & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -\mu E & -\mu M - \kappa\cdot\text{cav}
\end{pmatrix}
\]
(plus additional entries from mechanotransduction source terms).

\[
S =

5. Fluid equations (Navier–Stokes / ALE)
\[
\begin{align*}
\rho_f\Bigl(\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}\Bigr)
&= -\nabla p + \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau} + \mathbf{f}, \\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} &= 0.
\end{align*}
\]

6. Solid momentum balance (finite-strain)
\[
\rho_s\frac{\partial^2\boldsymbol{\chi}}{\partial t^2} = \nabla_{\mathbf{X}}\cdot\mathbf{P} + \mathbf{b}.
\]

7. Anisotropic multi-layer strain-energy density (HGO-type, scaled by \(E\))
For each layer \(\alpha\in\{\text{I},\text{M},\text{A}\}\):
\[
\Psi^\alpha(\mathbf{C},E) =
\frac{\mu^\alpha(E)}{2}(I_1-3)
\sum_i\frac{k_1^{\alpha,i}(E)}{2k_2^{\alpha,i}}
\Bigl\{\exp\bigl[k_2^{\alpha,i}(\kappa^\alpha I_1+(1-3\kappa^\alpha)I_4^i-1)^2\bigr]-1\Bigr\}
\Psi_{\rm vol}(J),
\]
with
\[
\mu^\alpha(E)=\mu_0^\alpha\,E,\qquad k_1^{\alpha,i}(E)=k_{1,0}^{\alpha,i}\,E.
\]

8. Fluid–structure interface conditions
\[
\mathbf{u}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\chi}}{\partial t},
\qquad
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{N}=-p\mathbf{n}+\boldsymbol{\tau}\mathbf{n}
\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{fs}(t).
\]

9. Weak-coupling block-triangular system
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal{F} & \mathcal{C}_{fs} & 0 \\
\mathcal{C}{sf} & \mathcal{S}{\rm aniso}(E^n) & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \mathcal{B}_{\rm RK}(J)
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathbf{u},p \\
\boldsymbol{\chi} \\
\mathbf{y}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathbf{f}_f \\
\mathbf{f}_s \\
\mathbf{f}_b(\overline{\tau}_w,\overline{\sigma},\overline{\varepsilon})
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

10. Cavitation criterion (Bernoulli estimate)
\[
p(\mathbf{x},t) < p_v + \tfrac12\rho|\mathbf{u}|^2.
\]

11. Rayleigh–Plesset bubble dynamics (optional micro-mechanical module)
\[
R_b\ddot{R}b + \tfrac32\dot{R}_b^2 = \frac{1}{\rho}\Bigl(p_g - p\infty - \frac{2\sigma}{R_b}-4\mu\frac{\dot{R}_b}{R_b}\Bigr).
\]

These are all the principal equations used in the formulations above.
